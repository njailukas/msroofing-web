About the service

A tile lasts for decades – if it's laid right

Clay and concrete tiles store heat well and last a very long time. They are heavier, though, so we always check the roof structure can carry them. After that, the result depends on precise battening, ventilation and how the ridge and valleys are finished.

Build-up

Tiled roof build-up

A tiled roof is a system where every layer has a job. These are the six layers we build under and with the tiles.

  1. 1

    Rafters

    The structure, which we check first. Tiles are heavier and the frame must carry them safely.

  2. 2

    Breathable membrane

    A vapour-permeable safety layer under the covering. Moisture gets out, rain stays out.

  3. 3

    Counter battens

    Create the ventilated gap through which moisture and heat escape from under the tiles.

  4. 4

    Battens

    We set the batten gauge exactly to the tile model. That's what makes a straight, watertight roof.

  5. 5

    Tiles

    Clay or concrete tiles laid in bond, clipped against wind uplift in exposed areas.

  6. 6

    Ridge tiles and vent roll

    Ridge closed with ridge tiles on a ventilated roll. Warm air escapes, rain and snow stay out.

A tile is only as good as the battens under it. That's why we measure and check the battening on every course.

Your questions

Clay or concrete tiles?

Clay tiles are a traditional ceramic covering with a very long life and a natural look. Concrete tiles are durable and usually more affordable. We're happy to compare both for your roof.

Can my roof structure carry tiles?

Tiles are heavier than metal or shingles. We always check the structure before laying them. If it can't carry them, we strengthen it or recommend a lighter covering.

What warranty do tiles have?

The material carries the manufacturer's warranty, up to 30–50 years for quality tiles. We give 10 years on our work.
